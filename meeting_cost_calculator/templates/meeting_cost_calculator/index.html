The red marker shows your current headcount. The curve shows how cost scales as you add more people — steeper than a straight line, because bigger groups don't just cost more per minute, they also run longer.

The base cost formula (headcount × hourly pay × duration) is the same one used by most published meeting-cost calculators. The "duration grows with headcount" model is this tool's own addition, motivated by two well-known findings: Fred Brooks's observation that communication channels between people grow much faster than the group itself ("The Mythical Man-Month", 1975), and Bain & Company's widely-cited research that decision-making effectiveness drops roughly 10% for each person added past about seven — part of why Amazon caps team meetings with its "two-pizza rule". The exact growth rate here is an adjustable estimate you control, not a precise measurement.
